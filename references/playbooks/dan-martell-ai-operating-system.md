## Frameworks (distilled)

**The AI Buyback Loop: Audit → Transfer → Fill.** Buying back time is a loop, not an event.
- *Audit:* pull last 7–14 days of calendar, color-code by energy (🟢 energizes / 🟡 neutral / 🔴 drains), mark each task as "should I be doing this?". Reds + low-value = transfer list. Goal: a calendar full of green.
- *Transfer:* Level 1 = Camcorder Method (record yourself doing the task, drop transcript into AI → it writes the SOP → a human follows it). Level 2 = transfer the *outcome* to AI (describe the result, let the agent do the whole task — no SOP, no hire).
- *Fill:* refill the freed time on three levels — Skills (upgrade AI skills; copy operators shipping real workflows), Habits (build one automation daily/weekly), Beliefs (upgrade your mental model of what one person can do).

**Buyback Rate** (his definition here): `(annual income ÷ 2,000) ÷ 4`. Delegate anything you could pay someone ~a quarter of your effective hourly rate to do. (Note: earlier casual version was just ÷2,000 — use his ÷4 number for the List B gate philosophy.)

**DRIP Matrix** — sort each task on money × energy: **D**elegate (low value + draining), **R**eplace (high value + draining), **I**nvest (energizing + low value today), **P**roduce (high value + energizing). Trend toward 95% Produce.

**AI Replacement Ladder** (climb in order, tokens before payroll at each rung):
1. **Admin → The Gatekeeper** — AI inbox/calendar manager. Highest-leverage rung. (= the FBCG Hub.)
2. **Delivery → The Concierge** — AI support/onboarding.
3. **Marketing → The Storyteller** — study outliers, remix with your angle.
4. **Sales → The Deal Maker** — chat-based selling, conversation summaries.
5. **Leadership → The Chief** — a shared "digital brain" the whole team queries instead of you.

## The Gatekeeper prompt (the crown jewel for the Hub)
Martell's inbox-manager process, in sequence:
1. **Pull everything** — read ALL email, do NOT pre-filter by Promotions/Social/Updates (filtering too early hides high-priority items).
2. **Strip the noise first** — auto-archive/label automated mail, spam, receipts, tool alerts (~60–65% of daily volume).
3. **Triage the rest by intent into 5 buckets:** Reply-me (needs my voice/decision → draft it) · Delegate (route to assistant w/ one-line instruction) · Monitor/FYI (one-line summary, no action) · Schedule (propose a calendar block) · No-reply (archive).
4. **Draft in my voice** using prior-thread context; direct, warm, no fluff. **Never send — draft only.**
5. **Present for review** as a table: Sender | Subject | Intent | Suggested action | Draft. Most immediate on top. Human approves, then sends.
- Rules: never send/schedule without approval; capture contacts/commitments/decisions to memory; when unsure, ask once then proceed.

## Prompt-engineering assets
- **RCCF format:** Role → Context (goal, audience, inputs, constraints) → Command (the action; give 3 options + a better path) → Format (output shape; end with CTA; run a QA).
- **Company Master Prompt:** paste once per chat to lock voice/style/priorities. Notable style rules in his template: short punchy sentences, tables to compare, "give me 3 options when choosing," "push back if my ask is weak," 3-point QA (Is it simple / useful today / next step clear), and **"straight quotes only, no em dashes."**

## 4-Part AI Deployment Playbook
Audit the business & expose bottlenecks (rank tasks by time + money) → Choose tools & build the workflow (**Trigger → AI → Automation → Human-in-the-loop**; AI takes 80–90% of repetitive work) → Test/automate/measure → Scale (standardize wins into SOPs, AI Champion per dept, reinvest gains).

**Metrics table (use for the Power Apps dashboard):** Time Saved (hours/task) · Cost (labor vs. automation) · Output Consistency (% approved without edits) · ROI = (Savings + Revenue Lift − AI Cost) ÷ AI Cost.
